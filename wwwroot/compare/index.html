Which one fits

PdfPecker is a document engine, not a browser. Pick something else when you need what only a browser does.

PdfPecker

Invoices, payslips, reports and other templates you control, when you want speed, a small deployment, no browser to keep patched and an MIT licence, with PDF/A, PDF/UA, signatures, forms and e-invoices built in.

HtmlRenderer.PdfSharp

The closest match: also fully managed, in process and free. Its HTML support stops at HTML 4 and CSS 2, so it suits simple layouts, and PDFsharp underneath gives you a solid API for drawing, merging and signing. PDFsharp 6.5 moved to AGPL or a paid licence. Moving from HtmlRenderer

PeachPDF

Also fully managed, in process and free, with a modern CSS engine, MathML, PDF 2.0, PDF/A-4 and a C# API for building documents in code, which can also draw pages of an existing PDF over or under them. It finds installed fonts on its own. In our tests it was 15 to 20 times slower than PdfPecker on an invoice or report, and about 7 times slower on a typical page, and it has no signing, merging or PDF to image.

IronPDF

Chrome-exact output with JavaScript, plus a large PDF editing API and paid support, if the licence fits your budget. Moving from IronPDF

Syncfusion or SelectPdf

Commercial PDF suites that drive Chrome for you, with JavaScript and a wide PDF API. Syncfusion is free for small companies under its community licence, and SelectPdf has a free edition for documents of up to 5 pages.

Aspose

A large commercial family of document APIs. Aspose.HTML renders the HTML with its own engine, and Aspose.PDF adds PDF/A, PDF/UA, signatures and the rest.

iText pdfHTML

A long-established PDF toolkit with deep standards support, under AGPL or a commercial licence. Complex scripts need its pdfCalligraph add-on.

PuppeteerSharp or Playwright

Free and exactly what Chrome prints, including JavaScript-heavy pages and live websites, at the cost of running a browser. Moving from Chrome

wkhtmltopdf

Archived since 2023 on a WebKit from 2016, so best kept for existing systems that already depend on it. Moving from wkhtmltopdf

QuestPDF, not HTML at all

Builds documents in C# code instead of HTML, with a well-designed layout API and a live preview app. Free for companies under USD 1 million in revenue, otherwise a yearly licence per company, and it ships native Skia libraries. With the same invoice and report rebuilt in it, PdfPecker was 2 to 4 times faster in our test, and QuestPDF needs every PDF/UA tag added in code. No signing, form fields or barcodes. To keep its style of API, PdfPecker.Fluent ports most QuestPDF code by changing the using lines.
