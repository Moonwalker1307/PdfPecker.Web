Which one fits

PdfPecker is a document engine, not a browser. Pick something else when you need what only a browser does.

PdfPecker

Invoices, payslips, reports and other templates you control, when you want speed, a small deployment, no browser to keep patched and an MIT licence, with PDF/A, PDF/UA, signatures, forms and e-invoices built in.

HtmlRenderer.PdfSharp

The closest match: also fully managed, in process and free. Its HTML support stops at HTML 4 and CSS 2, so it suits simple layouts, and PDFsharp underneath gives you a solid API for drawing, merging and signing. PDFsharp 6.5 moved to AGPL or a paid licence.

IronPDF

Chrome-exact output with JavaScript, plus a large PDF editing API and paid support, if the licence fits your budget.

Syncfusion or SelectPdf

Commercial PDF suites that drive Chrome for you, with JavaScript and a wide PDF API. Syncfusion is free for small companies under its community licence, and SelectPdf has a free edition for documents of up to 5 pages.

Aspose

A large commercial family of document APIs. Aspose.HTML renders the HTML with its own engine, and Aspose.PDF adds PDF/A, PDF/UA, signatures and the rest.

iText pdfHTML

A long-established PDF toolkit with deep standards support, under AGPL or a commercial licence. Complex scripts need its pdfCalligraph add-on.

PuppeteerSharp or Playwright

Free and exactly what Chrome prints, including JavaScript-heavy pages and live websites, at the cost of running a browser.

wkhtmltopdf

Archived since 2023 on a WebKit from 2016, so best kept for existing systems that already depend on it.

Not HTML at all

QuestPDF builds documents from C# code instead of HTML. It's a fine choice when you would rather write layout in code than keep HTML templates.
