    Right-to-left text, CJK and hyphenation

    Changed in 1.14.1: Hyphenation.

    Right-to-left text: Arabic and Hebrew

    Arabic and Hebrew are laid out right to left and shaped like browsers do, with PdfPecker's own implementation of the Unicode Bidirectional Algorithm (UAX #9, passing all of Unicode's conformance tests) and of OpenType shaping (GSUB and GPOS, following HarfBuzz). Arabic letters join, lam-alef and other ligatures form, and harakat and Hebrew points are placed on their letters by the font's mark positioning. Mixed text (an English product name or a number inside an Arabic sentence) is put in the right order, and brackets are mirrored. Register a font that has the script, then set the direction with the dir attribute or CSS direction:

    var options = new PdfOptions();
options.Fonts.AddFile("fonts/NotoNaskhArabic-Regular.ttf");
byte[] pdf = HtmlToPdf.Convert("""
    <html dir="rtl" lang="ar"><body style="font-family: 'Noto Naskh Arabic'">
      <h1>فاتورة رقم ٢٠٢٦/١٥</h1>
      <p>المبلغ الإجمالي: 1,250.00 DKK</p>
    </body></html>
    """, options);

    In a right-to-left block, text aligns right (text-align: start), and table columns, flex rows, grid columns and list markers run from the right. dir="auto", <bdi> and <bdo> work as in browsers, and so do unicode-bidi, text-align: start/end and the logical margin and padding properties (margin-inline-start, padding-inline-end, ...). text-overflow: ellipsis and line-clamp cut right-to-left lines at their left end. SVG <text> is reordered and shaped too, with direction deciding which way a text chunk runs from its x. Copied or extracted text gives back the original characters in logical order. A font without OpenType Arabic features is shaped with the Unicode presentation forms instead.

    Indic, Thai and Lao scripts

    Devanagari, Bengali, Gurmukhi, Gujarati, Oriya, Tamil, Telugu, Kannada and Malayalam are shaped as HarfBuzz shapes them, from a port of its Indic shaper: conjuncts and half forms join, reph moves above its cluster and pre-base vowel signs are drawn before their consonant. Thai and Lao vowels and tone marks stack correctly, and combining marks in Latin, Greek and Cyrillic text are placed on their letters, or replaced by the font's precomposed letter. Register a font that has the script, such as one of the Noto fonts:

    var options = new PdfOptions();
options.Fonts.AddFile("fonts/NotoSansDevanagari-Regular.ttf");
byte[] pdf = HtmlToPdf.Convert("<p lang='hi' style=\"font-family: 'Noto Sans Devanagari'\">कुल राशि: ₹1,250</p>", options);

    Khmer, Myanmar, Sinhala, Tibetan, Mongolian, Javanese, Adlam and Syriac are not shaped yet (see Limitations).

    Chinese, Japanese and Korean line breaking

    Chinese and Japanese are written without spaces, so a line may break between almost any two characters. PdfPecker finds those breaks with the Unicode Line Breaking Algorithm (UAX #14) and the Japanese kinsoku rules, as browsers do: a line never starts with closing punctuation such as 。, 、 or 」, or with an iteration mark such as 々, and never ends with an opening bracket or quotation mark. Nothing is drawn at these breaks, and Latin words, numbers and emoji sequences inside the text stay whole. Text in other scripts breaks at spaces and hyphens, as before. The standard and bundled fonts have no CJK characters, so register a font that has them:

    var options = new PdfOptions();
options.Fonts.AddFile("fonts/NotoSansJP-Regular.ttf");
byte[] pdf = HtmlToPdf.Convert("""
    <html lang="ja"><body style="font-family: 'Noto Sans JP'; line-break: strict">
      <p>請求書は月末までにお支払いください。ご不明な点がございましたら、お気軽にお問い合わせください。</p>
    </body></html>
    """, options);

    CSS line-break sets how strict the rules are, as in Chrome:

    • auto (the default) and strict keep small kana (ゃ, ッ, ...) and the prolonged sound mark ー off the start of a line.
    • normal lets small kana and ー start a line, and allows a break before the hyphens ‐, –, 〜 and ゠.
    • loose also allows a break before an iteration mark or centred punctuation such as ・ and ：, and on both sides of an ellipsis.
    • anywhere breaks between any two characters and ignores the punctuation rules, in every script.

    Korean text breaks between its syllables by default, as in Chrome. word-break: keep-all breaks CJK text only at spaces and punctuation instead, which suits Korean, written with spaces between words. word-break: break-all and white-space: nowrap work as for any other text. A justified line is stretched only at its spaces, so a Chinese or Japanese line without spaces is not justified (see Limitations).

    Hyphenation Changed in 1.14.1

    hyphens: auto hyphenates words with the TeX hyphenation patterns (Liang's algorithm) of the text's language: the nearest lang attribute, else PdfOptions.Language. As in Chrome, a word is hyphenated only where it does not fit on its line, and a hyphen (or the hyphenate-character) is shown at the break. Words shorter than five letters, words with digits or other non-letters inside, URLs and e-mail addresses, and capitalized English words are not hyphenated, and a word with &shy; breaks only there. French and Italian also hyphenate words with an elided article or pronoun, such as l'ordinateur in French or l'informazione in Italian, with a straight or typographic apostrophe.

    hyphenate-limit-chars sets the shortest word that is hyphenated and the fewest letters before and after a hyphen: up to three values, each auto or a whole number, such as hyphenate-limit-chars: 8 3 3. One value sets only the word length; with two, the letters after a hyphen are the same as before it. It is inherited, so it can be set on the whole document or on a single element. auto keeps the defaults above: words of at least five letters, and the patterns' own minimums around a hyphen (two letters before and three after in English, as in Chrome). Larger values make hyphenation more selective; smaller values do not go below the patterns' own minimums, as in Chrome.

    Patterns for these languages are built in and loaded on first use, for every tag of the language (fr-CA, pt-BR, es-MX and so on):

    Language Tags
    English (US patterns) en
    Danish da
    German (reformed spelling) de
    Dutch nl
    Norwegian nb, nn, no
    Swedish sv
    Spanish es
    French fr
    Italian it
    Portuguese pt
    Polish pl

    Others can be supplied as TeX pattern files, such as those of the hyph-utf8 project:

    var options = new PdfOptions
{
    // Your own patterns for a language (they take precedence over the built-in ones).
    Hyphenation = new Dictionary<string, HyphenationPatterns>
    {
        ["fi"] = new HyphenationPatterns(File.ReadAllText("hyph-fi.tex"), leftMin: 2, rightMin: 2),
    },
};
byte[] pdf = HtmlToPdf.Convert("""
    <html lang="da"><body style="hyphens: auto; text-align: justify">
      <p>Arbejdsmarkedsbidraget betales af lønmodtageren.</p>
      <p lang="fi">Tavutus omilla tavutussäännöillä.</p>
    </body></html>
    """, options);
