    This is the manual for PdfPecker 1.14.1. Read it for the latest version, 1.18.0.

    Limitations

    PdfPecker is built for fast conversion of HTML templates you control, not for rendering arbitrary web pages. These are the known gaps; most are scope choices for speed and size, and they are the first candidates for future work. Anything not listed in What is supported should be assumed missing.

    By design

    • No JavaScript. Scripts are never run, so content must be in the HTML before conversion (render it server-side first).
    • No network access: http:/https: stylesheets and images are not downloaded. Supply them through your ResourceResolver, as files under BaseDirectory, or as data: URIs.
    • <canvas>, <iframe>, <video>, <audio>, <object> and <embed> are not drawn, nor are file, range, colour and image inputs. Form fields have no JavaScript (no calculations, formats or submit actions), and a field inside a transformed box, a running header or footer, or a position: fixed box is drawn but not fillable.

    Text and fonts

    • Without registered fonts or EmbedStandardFonts, text uses the standard PDF fonts, so characters are limited to the Windows-1252 set (Western European languages); other characters are replaced with ?. The bundled Liberation fonts add Central European, Greek and Cyrillic, but not CJK or other scripts: register fonts for those.
    • Complex-script shaping covers Arabic and Hebrew (and other scripts written with Arabic letters, such as Persian and Urdu), nine Indic scripts, Thai and Lao. Khmer, Myanmar, Sinhala, Tibetan, Mongolian, Javanese, Adlam and Syriac are not shaped, so their vowel signs and conjuncts come out wrong. Thai and Lao text breaks only at spaces, since there is no dictionary for word breaks. There is no vertical text (writing-mode).
    • Chinese, Japanese and Korean text breaks into lines, but text-align: justify stretches only spaces, so CJK lines without spaces are not justified (text-justify is not supported), and punctuation is not compressed at the start or end of a line.
    • In Latin and other simple scripts, shaping is limited to kerning, standard ligatures and features that swap one glyph for another (see OpenType features); contextual alternates and other features that depend on neighbouring characters are not applied, and small-caps is synthesized from scaled capitals.
    • Automatic hyphenation (hyphens: auto) has built-in patterns for English, Danish, German, Dutch, Norwegian, Swedish, Spanish, French, Italian, Portuguese and Polish; other languages need their patterns in PdfOptions.Hyphenation.
    • SVG <text> uses only the first value of position lists and has no textPath; in a text chunk with right-to-left text, dx on its tspans is ignored.

    CSS

    • Container queries: no scroll-state() queries, style() works only on custom properties, and style containment (counter scoping) and the contain property are not implemented. A document with container queries takes about two to four times as long to lay out, and queries that keep changing the layout stop after six passes. In running headers and footers, @page margin boxes and SVG images, the cq units fall back to viewport units.
    • Footnotes: @footnote inside @page :first, :left or :right is ignored, footnote-policy: block acts like line, and footnote-display: compact is always inline. A note split across pages does not carry its background or border onto the next page, and margins between block notes do not collapse.
    • When @page :first, :left or :right give pages different widths, floats, inline-blocks, flex, grid and multi-column containers, and boxes with transforms or filters keep the width of the page they start on (Chrome re-sizes floats, inline-blocks and multi-column per page). A named page's :first is the document's first page, as in Chrome, not the first page of the named run.
    • Selectors: a rule with an unsupported selector is skipped. A complex selector may have at most 64 compound parts and 16 levels of nesting, and :has() cannot contain :has().
    • Blurred text shadows are drawn as images at 144 dpi (down to 72 dpi for wide blurs, with the blur capped at about 200px), so shadowing long body text adds to the file size; the shadow of an underline is painted under the glyphs rather than between them and their own underline. filter: blur() and drop-shadow() draw the box as an image; its text stays selectable, and the colour filters keep everything as vectors.
    • text-overflow applies only at the end of the line, as in the spec and Chrome.
    • Percentage heights still count as auto in a flexed item of a container that has only a min-height, against a table row's height on its own or a rowspanning cell, and in footnotes and running headers and footers. When pages differ in size, a box resolves its percentage height against the page it starts on and keeps that height if it breaks onto a page of another height (Chrome recomputes it there).
    • background-attachment: fixed scrolls with the box, since paper has no scrolling viewport.

    Backgrounds and transparency

    • Borders of inline elements (<span>) are not drawn, and neither are their inset box shadows. box-decoration-break applies to inline elements, not to blocks split across pages or columns.
    • Inline boxes nested more than 16 deep are not decorated, and one layout paints at most 50,000 inline box fragments with a shared budget for their layers, shadows and outlines; past it, fragments show only their background colour.
    • A translucent box shadow without blur is blended onto white rather than truly see-through. Blurs are drawn as bands, so very wide blurs may show faint steps when zoomed in.

    Layout

    • Subgrid: percentage gaps, margins and padding on a subgrid resolve against the parent grid's width rather than the subgrid's area; subgrid items are not baseline-aligned with the parent's items; and only the horizontal writing-mode is supported.
    • Multi-column: column-span: all works only on direct children of the multi-column box, and multi-column boxes nested more than two levels deep keep their children whole instead of splitting them. Footnotes inside columns are placed at their call and do not reserve space at the bottom of the column, orphans and widows are not applied to lines beside floats, and content that overflows a fixed-height multi-column box with overflow: hidden is clipped from view but stays in the PDF's text.
    • Overflow: a positioned child with a z-index inside a clipping box stays stacked within that box.

    Images and SVG

    • No AVIF images: decoding them needs an AV1 video decoder, which is not planned. Convert them to WebP, PNG or JPEG.
    • SVG filters are applied in pixels at 144 dpi, so filtered shapes become an image (text keeps an invisible, selectable copy).

    Reading and changing existing PDFs

    • Text extraction reads columns that sit side by side across, line by line, rather than one column after the other, and it does not use the structure tree of a tagged PDF to find the reading order. Text drawn only as outlines or images is not extracted; there is no OCR. Indic vowel signs drawn before their consonant come out in that visual order unless the PDF carries /ActualText.
    • Form filling does not shape text, so values in Arabic, Hebrew, Devanagari and other scripts that need shaping are stored but not drawn correctly. XFA forms are not filled: their XFA data is removed and the AcroForm fields are filled instead. Field JavaScript (calculations, formats) is not run.
    • Signature verification counts the updates made after a signature but does not analyse them against its permissions (no DocMDP or FieldMDP change analysis), so it cannot tell an allowed form fill from a changed page. Revocation is checked only when RevocationMode asks the platform to, not from the OCSP responses and CRLs stored in the PDF.

    Markdown and the PDF renderer

    • Markdown: no math. Definition list terms are one line each (no several terms for one definition), and ~ does not start a definition. A footnote's text cannot hold another page-bottom footnote: a reference inside a footnote links to its note, and a note that only other notes reference is listed after the text.
    • PdfRenderer (PDF to image): no predefined CJK CMaps or non-embedded CJK fonts, and ICC profiles are not applied (ICC-based colours use their device equivalents). WebP output is lossless only (no lossy VP8), and JPEG output is baseline only (no progressive or arithmetic coding).
